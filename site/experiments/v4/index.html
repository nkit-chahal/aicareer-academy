Hiring · 2021–2027 sketch

The curve that actually moves careers in India — and the three people still arguing with it.

Bengaluru still writes the thickest offer letters. NCR and Hyderabad are filling GCC floors. Pune keeps the analytics shops running. None of that is a doorway to “course in [city]”. The useful split is who you are: a career switcher, a software engineer, or an analyst.

Four metros as weather, not as landing pages

If you work in data or AI in India, you already know the map. Demand clusters. It does not politely distribute itself across SEO URLs. We teach from Gurugram and online; the cities below are how the labour market talks — context for the chart, not a directory.

Bengaluru

Chapter set

Three people. Same hiring curve. Different homework.

The chart does not care about your motivation quote. It cares whether you can do a specific kind of work. Here is how the same India market reads if you are switching fields, already writing production software, or already trusted with the numbers.

01

The career switcher

Seven years in operations, sales, or a non-CS degree. Sharp. Tired of being told to “just learn Python”.

You are not late. You are also not a hidden machine-learning engineer. India’s hiring curve through 2026 still has room for people who can own a business problem, wrangle messy data, and use modern models without theatre. What it does not have room for is a six-week miracle.

The switch is real when you can show a before-and-after on a problem a manager already has — not when you finish a certificate.

What to build: SQL you can defend, a first model you can explain, an LLM workflow with evaluation, and a portfolio that names the industry you came from.

02

The software engineer

Backend, full-stack, or mobile. You already ship. You are bored of being handed a “prompting” slide deck.

Bengaluru and Hyderabad will keep hiring engineers who can put models behind APIs, watch latency, and not set the bill on fire. Your gap is rarely syntax. It is retrieval, evals, orchestration, and the unglamorous work of making an agent survive contact with production data.

You do not need another beginner Python week. You need systems literacy for models — the same seriousness you already have for databases.

What to build: RAG that fails loudly, traces, eval sets, and a service you would actually on-call.

03

The analyst

Excel, SQL, dashboards. BFSI, ops, or a GCC reporting team. People already ask you “what happened?”

Pune and NCR are full of this person. The hiring curve past 2026 does not erase analysts; it asks them to go from describing last quarter to shaping the next one — with models, experiments, and language models that are checked, not worshipped.

You already have the rare part: business instinct. The missing part is a stack that lets you move from slide to system.

What to build: a metric you own, a forecast with a stated error, and an assisted-analysis workflow a risk team could audit.
