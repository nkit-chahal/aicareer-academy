Chart of the decade

The jobs arrived first.
The sequence came later.

From 2018 through 2026, postings for analytics, data science, and AI roles did not move as one line. Analytics climbed steadily. Data-science titles boomed, then split. AI titles bent upward after generative models left the lab. If you are in India deciding what to learn next, the chart is the argument — not a course list.

01 · How to read it

Three titles. Three slopes.

The vertical axis is a relative demand index, not openings in lakhs and not “seats filled.” Analytics is the volume job: dashed, stubborn, still rising. Data science is the title that defined the late 2010s. AI is the line that waited, then ran.

02 · 2018

The floor was already analytics.

Before “prompt” was a job skill, companies were hiring people who could join tables and explain a number to a manager. That work did not vanish when models got famous. It is still the widest on-ramp.

03 · 2020–21

Digital work pulled the whole chart up.

Remote operations, e-commerce, and reporting stacks thickened demand for SQL, Python, and dashboards. Data-science titles rode the same wave — often as a premium label on work that was still mostly analysis plus a model.

04 · 2023

Generative AI bent one line, not all three.

After large language models became a consumer product, postings that said AI, ML, LLM, or GenAI steepened. Data-scientist as a catch-all cooled and split: some work stayed science; some was relabelled engineering. The chart’s dip-then-rise on the black line is that renaming, not a disappearance of the work.

05 · 2026

You are choosing on a split market.

Today the red line is loud. The dashed line still hires more people. A career is not “AI versus analytics.” It is which slope you can actually walk — given the Python you have, and the systems you are willing to own.

06 · Beyond 2026

The projection is a band, not a promise.

Past 2026 the lines continue as a sketch: AI titles stay elevated, analytics keeps compounding, data science remains a real job if it means experiments and models rather than a synonym for Excel. The shaded band is uncertainty. We are not counting our students on this axis.

India

What the chart means if you live here

The index is not an India-only census. Job-posting series mix global product firms, consultancies, and — increasingly — capability centres in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, and the NCR. For a person in Gurugram or anywhere on that map, the useful reading is directional: the work exists, the titles moved, and the entry ticket is still boring on purpose.

Services firms still need analysts who can trust a pipeline. Product companies and GCCs need people who can put a model behind an API, or retrieve the right document without hallucinating a policy. Those are different jobs. The chart’s three lines are a reminder not to buy a single slogan — “learn AI” — and expect it to place you on every slope at once.

The scarce skill is not a tool name. It is a sequence you can finish: data, then judgement, then a system someone else can run.

Wages and “guaranteed placement” figures do not belong on this page. They vary by city, degree, years of work, and whether the role is captive GCC or services. What we can say without inventing a percentage: Python is the shared first step; SQL still interviews; production AI is a second language, not a replacement for the first.

  • Volume Analytics remains the broadest hire. If you need a first data role, start on the dashed line — then specialise.
  • Titles “Data scientist” now splits toward research-flavoured work or toward ML/LLM engineering. Read the JD, not the fashion.
  • AI work The steep line is real. It mostly pays people who can evaluate, retrieve, serve, and observe — not only chat with a model.

Sources informing the shape of the chart: public job-posting indexes and emerging-jobs write-ups (LinkedIn, Indeed and Indian boards as reported in secondary summaries), NASSCOM technology and skills notes, World Economic Forum Future of Jobs discussions of AI and data roles. This page does not reprint their tables, and does not treat them as AI Career Academy outcomes.
